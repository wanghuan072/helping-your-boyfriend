Inside the story

We start inside a relationship.
Not at the beginning of one.

Our boyfriend is a doctor, and we already share a life with him. A leg illness keeps our character at home. We wake late, work on the dolls Adrian brings back, and decide how to answer someone who seems to know us so well.

The pastel rooms and affectionate conversation make the routine feel safe. The doll work asks us to slow down, watch the tools, and notice what changes. We follow that first night before opening the later route conditions.

Our first night

The sweetest routines can be
the hardest ones to question.

Adrian comes home late. We answer his questions, take apart the dolls and settle into a relationship that already feels familiar. The uneasy part is not meeting a stranger—it is deciding what to believe about someone close to us.

Read the room. Then read the prompt.

Dialogue keys and doll-work controls do different jobs. We follow the current tool instruction, watch the counters, and keep a separate save for retries rather than rushing back through the whole conversation.

What changes on a four-ending replay?

Early replies, investigation steps and late decisions belong to different checkpoints. We choose a direction before overwriting a working save.

Where does Adrian fit in the story?

He is both our boyfriend and a doctor, tied to the care routine and the doll work. Later characters have separate spoiler-marked profiles.
